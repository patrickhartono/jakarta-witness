# Jakarta Witness — Artist Statement

*Patrick Hartono, 2026 — draft (~300 words)*

---

Jakarta does not resolve into data. It is one of the densest, most
improvised cities on earth: a place where millions of motorbikes, street
vendors, ojek drivers and pedestrians negotiate space through an informal
choreography that no traffic model has ever fully described. Jakarta
Witness asks what happens when a machine tries anyway.

The work places a viewer inside a single act of machine cognition,
unfolding in three layers. At the top is *reality* — real Jakarta traffic
footage, overlaid with the restless bounding boxes of a neural network
naming what it sees. In the middle is *thought* — a language model that
observes the scene and, in a cold and clinical voice, proposes a
solution. At the bottom is *reduction* — a flickering 2D simulation in
which the city has become colored rectangles sliding along a grid.

The machine is articulate and confident. Its solutions are logical: ban
the motorbikes, meter the cars, lift the traffic onto an elevated road.
Each is also quietly catastrophic, because each is reasoned purely from
counts and densities and knows nothing of the lives those counts stand
for. The bottom simulation makes this loss visible. A city of millions
flattens into a handful of boxes. That flattening is not a technical
shortcoming to be apologised for — it is the subject of the work.

Jakarta Witness is built entirely from open, browser-native AI: nothing
is sent to a server, nothing is cloud-processed. The intelligence on
display is small, local, and exposed. Watching it think, we are invited
to notice the moment where measurement becomes confidence, and confidence
becomes a plan — and to ask who is standing in the gap between the data
and the city it claims to know.
